# Skyward Aces

A 3D browser dogfighting game, from WW1 biplanes to modern fighter jets. It uses three.js with no build step: open `index.html` in Chrome, Edge or Firefox.

## Menus
- **Top bar**: research XP, coins, and the pilot profile (level, XP to the next level, K/D ratio and win rate from multiplayer battles only, singleplayer stats shown separately, settings). Each level needs 1.1× the XP of the one before it.
- **Play**: Singleplayer, Multiplayer or Tutorials. Singleplayer and Multiplayer offer four modes:
  - **Destroy**: two teams of 1–60 (some bots fly bombers and attack aircraft). Kills 3, bases 2/3/5, tanks 0.5. Most points at the end wins, or win early by destroying every enemy base, or by destroying the enemy's airfields (they can no longer respawn) and shooting down every enemy aircraft. Kills score 3; small, medium and big bases score 2, 3 and 5. The host sets the match length. The team with the most points wins.
  - **Dogfight**: 1v1 up to 8v8, with 0–12 respawns each. The last team flying wins.
  - **Missions**: historical missions for the nation of your aircraft, closest era first. Mission types are fighter sweeps, bomber intercepts, ground strikes and bomber escorts.
  - **Survival**: co-op waves of enemy fighters, with bombers every third wave.
- **Tutorials** (`js/tutorial.js`): thirteen guided lessons in two groups. **Basic Game Mechanics**: flight basics, air gunnery, level bombing with the Norden bombsight (B-17G), dive bombing (Ju 87), jet bombing with CCIP (F-16C), rockets (P-47D), air-to-air missiles (F-16C, each missile type on its own key) and missile defence (Typhoon: flares and a hard turn against heat seekers, notching and chaff against a radar missile). **Basic Flight Maneuvers**: take-off, landing, turns (level turn, break turn, chandelle), rolls and the loop, the Immelmann and the split-S. Each lesson gives you a suitable aircraft, whether you own it or not; once completed, it can be flown again with any aircraft you own that can do it. There are no rewards, apart from the tutorial achievements.
- **Multiplayer**: the host clicks *Generate join code* and shares the code; other players enter it under *Join*. The host picks the mode and settings, can move players between teams, and chooses whether empty slots are filled with bots. Bots fly the average era of the human players. If a player drops out mid-battle, a bot takes over their aircraft.
- **Hangar**: a tab for each nation (with its flag), aircraft grouped by era with a picture and type (fighter, heavy fighter, dive bomber, heavy bomber, strike aircraft...), full stats and a 3D preview.
- **Modifications**: ammunition belts per gun; ordnance per pylon; airframe upgrades. Everything bought can be fitted and removed freely.
  - **Pylon diagram:** a War Thunder-style row of numbered pylons over a top view of the aircraft, from the left wingtip through the centreline to the right wingtip. Click a pylon and pick from the bombs, rockets, missiles and gun pods that fit it. Fitting is limited by pylon type, weight per pylon, total payload, era and nation, and can be mirrored onto the other wing. Hardpoint groups are expanded into individual pylons by real position (`js/loadout.js`); internal bomb bays stay one slot.
  - **Paint schemes** (`js/liveries.js`): 36 alternative schemes that the types really wore, each bought once per aircraft with coins and switched freely. Other players see them. Examples: USAAF natural metal, US Navy tri-colour and Glossy Sea Blue, RAF desert and Special Night, Luftwaffe RLM 70/71, tropical RLM 79/78, winter whitewash and RLM 81/82, Richthofen's red, the A6M2's amber grey, Soviet winter white and AMT-4/6, Black Arrows black, Hill Gray II, European I, JASDF aggressor blue and Operation Daguet sand.
  - **Ordnance by real designation:** bombs include the P.u.W., Gros-Andreau, AN-M30 to AN-M66, SC 50 to SC 1800 "Satan", the 4000 lb "Cookie", the FAB and Type 98/99 series, Mk 81-84, Snakeye, CBU-87, BL755, Belouga, Paveway, JDAM, KAB-500 and AASM. Rockets include Tiny Tim, HVAR, RP-3, R4M, WGr 21, S-5/S-8/S-13/S-24, SNEB and CRV7. Missiles run from the AIM-4 and AIM-9B to the AIM-120D, AIM-54 Phoenix, R-40, R-27, R-77-1, Magic, MICA, ASRAAM and Meteor.
- **Research**: spend XP on aircraft — fighters, bombers and attack aircraft (140 in all). In each nation you can research up to one era above the newest era you own in that nation. Every nation starts with one WW1 aircraft.
- **Achievements**: 51 achievements with progress bars, in four tiers that pay 2,500 / 8,000 / 25,000 / 75,000 coins and 500 / 2,000 / 6,000 / 20,000 XP when unlocked.
- **Leaderboard**: built without a server. In a multiplayer lobby (and again after the battle) the games swap pilot cards (name, level, multiplayer battles, wins, kills, deaths), each passing on the cards it already knows, so the board grows with every player you meet. A card shows what that player's own game reports.
- **Rewards**: XP and coins for kills, assists, damage, bases and winning. Assists add to your own score but never to the team score. Leaving a battle early forfeits the participation reward: you keep only what you earned.

## Maps, weather and time of day
Every mode lets the host pick the map (Countryside, Coastline, Pacific Islands, Desert, Winter, Alpine Valleys, Canyonlands, Farmland, Jungle Rivers, Arctic Sea, or random), its size (small / medium / large / massive: four times the battle area each way, about 100 km across for modern jets, with the bases and spawns still in the battle area), the weather (clear, scattered clouds, overcast, rain, thunderstorm with lightning, snowfall, fog, or random) and night. Bad weather lowers visibility and brings turbulence; at night the bases and runways are lit.

## Tanks
Destroy battles have tanks from each team driving around their territory (era-appropriate models). Rifle-calibre rounds barely scratch them; heavy machine guns, cannon (best with AP rounds), rockets and bombs kill them. Each tank is worth 0.5 points.

## Flying
Gunnery assist (Profile settings, on by default): put the aim circle on the red lead marker and the instructor tracks it; the marker turns into a white FIRE cue when your guns are on target and in range. Arrows at the screen edge point to nearby enemies off screen. Bot skill can be set from Rookie to Ace.

The mouse-aim autopilot works like War Thunder's instructor: it flies the aircraft towards the aim circle with damped, rate-based inputs, keeps the angle of attack below the stall and eases off when the pilot is greying out. The manual pitch keys override all of that. The bank it uses follows how far the aim is off to the side: a small correction is a shallow bank, and only a big turn (or an aim that keeps sweeping, like a turning target) is flown on its side or rolled over.
- **Post-stall manoeuvres:** high-AoA jets (Su-27, MiG-29, F/A-18) and thrust-vectoring jets (F-22, Su-35, Su-57) can be hauled far past the stall with the manual pull: a Pugachev's Cobra (pull hard at ~400 km/h and let go) bleeds a lot of speed; holding it on a thrust-vectoring jet flips it into a Kulbit. Other aircraft simply stall.
- **G-forces:** above the pilot's tolerance the view narrows and greys (no G-suit before the jet age: ~4.5-5 G; G-suits: 5.5-7.5 G). Pull ~2 G beyond that and it builds up to G-LOC: the screen goes black and the pilot is unconscious for ~5 s. Strong negative G reddens the view. Bots feel G too.

## Cockpit view
Press C for the cockpit: an interior fixed to the aircraft with the real nose and wings ahead. WW1 open cockpits (ring-and-bead sight, gun breeches), WW2 framed canopies with reflector gunsights and round dials, early-jet gyro sights, and modern HUDs with multi-function displays. The instruments are live.

## Controls
All controls can be rebound in **Profile → Controls** (keys or mouse buttons). Defaults:
| | |
|---|---|
| Mouse | aim — the aircraft banks and pulls towards the circle (small moves = small, smooth corrections). Aiming below the nose pushes it down with the wings level (to about -1.5 to -2 G); only aiming more than ~50° below rolls over and pulls, as in a split-S |
| Left mouse | guns (and gun pods) |
| Right mouse (hold) | zoom in |
| V (hold) | look around |
| W / S | throttle; hold W at 100% for WEP or afterburner |
| A / D | roll (ramps in smoothly; let go and the aircraft holds that bank in a level turn until you move the mouse) |
| Q / E | rudder |
| Arrow down / up | manual pull / push: bypasses the autopilot's stall and G protection (stalls, spins, cobra) |
| 1-8 | weapons: every type carried has its own key, in the order of the weapons line (heat-seeking missiles, radar missiles, bombs, rockets), e.g. 1 = AIM-9, 2 = AIM-120, 3 = Mk 82. Missiles fire at their own seeker's lock; bombs drop from the outboard pylons first, in mirrored pairs |
| X | countermeasures: flares, plus chaff on Cold War and later jets |
| B | bombsight view (level bombers with a bomb aimer) |
| Z / H | flaps down / up a notch (combat, take-off, landing; too fast and they tear off) |
| Space | air brake in flight (jets, dive bombers), wheel brakes on the ground |
| T | switch guns: all / machine guns only / cannons only |
| Middle mouse (hold) | keep the camera on your target |
| G | landing gear (Realistic Mode) |
| C | chase / nose camera |
| Tab | scoreboard |
| Esc | pause / leave |

The crosshair colour can be changed in the profile settings.

## Bombsights
Level bombers with a bomb aimer have their documented sight. Press B to look through it:
- **Sights:** Norden M-9 (B-17G, B-29, B-25J), Lotfernrohr 7 / 7C / 7K (He 111, Do 17, Ju 88, Ar 234), Mark XIV (Lancaster), Course Setting Bomb Sight (Blenheim, Hart, DH.9A, O/400), Goerz (Gotha), OPB-5S (Il-28), OPB-11R (Tu-16) and T2 (Canberra). Others are described by their class.
- **View:** the telescope looks forward and down along the dropping angle, magnified about 4×, so the cross wires mark where a bomb released now lands. Gyro-stabilised sights stay level while you manoeuvre; fixed sights roll with the aircraft. Each sight has its own graticule (fine cross hairs, the Mark XIV "sword", drift wires).
- **Readings:** height, air and ground speed, drift, dropping angle and time of fall. There is no on-target cue.

## Realistic Mode
A checkbox in every game mode. There is no instructional text on screen (the tutorials teach take-off and landing) and no bomb pipper, except on 4th-generation jets, which really have CCIP. Warning banners are replaced by what the aircraft itself has: voice warning systems on the F-16C, F/A-18E, F-22, Typhoon, Rafale, Mirage 2000, MiG-29 and the Flankers, in English, French or Russian, spoken by the browser's speech synthesiser; and radar-warning tones on jets. You start on your team's airfield runway with the engine idling: throttle up and pull up at the speed the HUD shows. Raise the gear after take-off. Lower it to land; a gentle touchdown on the wheels (wings level, sink under ~6 m/s) is a landing, 6-9 m/s is a hard landing that damages the airframe, more is a crash. Stop at your own airfield for 12 seconds to refuel, rearm and repair. Airfields can be bombed: a team whose airfields are all destroyed can no longer take off. Bots take off from the runway too.

## Sound
Real recordings, listed with author and licence in `assets/sounds/CREDITS.md` and under *Profile → Sound credits*:
- **Engines:** a Rolls-Royce Merlin, a radial and a propeller cruise loop for piston aircraft, and a Saab Draken for jets.
- **Guns:** rifle-calibre guns use recordings of their real cartridges (.30-06, 7.62×54R and Japanese 7.7 mm, from the CC0 Free Firearm Sound Library). Heavier guns and cannon play recorded reports over synthesised bodies.
- **Explosions and fly-bys:** real explosions, and close fly-bys use a real Spitfire or Draken pass.

`tools/fetch_sounds.mjs` and `tools/prepare_shot.mjs` rebuild the sound files. The recordings are layered with synthesis: piston engines buzz at their real cylinder firing frequency (rotary, inline and radial engines differ, multi-engine aircraft beat), jets have a roar, turbine whine and afterburner rumble, guns fire at their real rate of fire with a report per calibre (Gatling guns growl), distant sounds arrive late at the speed of sound and lose their highs, and nearby aircraft fly past with a Doppler shift.

## How the simulation works
- **Aircraft models** (`js/airframe.js`, `js/aircraft3d.js`): `airframe.js` is a strict registry with one explicit entry per aircraft and no class defaults. `Airframe.spec()` validates every entry and throws on any missing or contradictory property, such as canted fins without a cant angle, canards without a length, or an unknown intake profile. Entries hold published data (length, span, height, wing area: the root chord is computed from the wing area, and the fin height of tricycle aircraft from the height), the fuselage `fuselageLengthToWidthRatio` and depth ratio, `wingMountHeight` (0 low .. 1 high), wing planform / sweep / taper / dihedral, `intakeProfile` (`side_box`, `chin_scoop`, `nose_cone`, `cheek_crescent`, plus `ventral_box`, `wing_root`, `nacelle`, `none`) with real mouth sizes in metres, `tailConfiguration` (`single_vertical`, `twin_vertical_straight`, `twin_vertical_canted` with its `finCantDeg`, `t_tail`, `twin_endplate`, `twin_boom`), canards (`canardPresence`, `canardLength`, `canardSweep`, cockpit-relative offset), engines, cockpit and an explicit list of extras. Each entry also has a `fuselageArchitecture`, assigned from the real engine and tail layout by `node tools/audit_specs.mjs --write`. There are three: `SINGLE_TUBE` (one central fuselage); `TWIN_NACELLE_DECK` (F-15, F/A-18E, MiG-29, Su-27 family: a narrow cockpit pod and two separate engine tunnels from the intake mouths to the nozzles, joined by a flat deck, with the twin fins on the tunnel tops and a sharp LEX shelf when `lexPresence` is set); and `CHINED_STEALTH` (F-22, Su-57: a hard-edged hexagonal section whose chine runs from the nose through caret intakes into the wing roots). `aircraft3d.js` builds closed solids only (lofted superellipse bodies, NACA-section surfaces, closed lathe rings) through a 4-stage pipeline. (1) Core fuselage by architecture: each station is a set of superellipse primitives (core or pod, intake trunks, engine tunnels, a solid bridging deck, engine housings) lofted as one skin through a smooth union, so the parts weld together with fillets and the deck / stealth centreline is solid from the intakes to the tail. (2) Jet fuselages stop blunt at the exhaust plane, with a tail boom above the jets where the nozzles are short of the tail (F-4). Every engine ends in an open tailpipe: a converging-diverging nozzle (afterburner petals, a dark inner wall, the flame holder and turbine cone) that stands proud of the body by its registry `nozzleLength`. The real exposed length is taken from photos, for example 1.1 m on the F-15 and 1.3 m on the Su-27. Each jet also has an explicit `nozzleStyle`: a `plain` fixed tailpipe, a `feathered` afterburner with scalloped turkey-feather petals, `bare`, as on USAF and JASDF F-15s, whose feathers were removed so the actuator rods are visible, or one of the stealth styles. `2d_serrated` (F-22) is a flat 2.5 : 1 vectoring slot whose wedge-shaped upper and lower flaps end in sawtooth chevrons. `round_serrated` (Su-57) is a round vectoring nozzle with sawtooth petal edges. On `CHINED_STEALTH` bodies the flat top and bottom decks taper over the last ~3 m onto the nozzle flaps, or onto a slim tail stinger when the engines are in nacelles, so there is no flat rear wall. The fuselage skin stops `nozzleLength` short of the published length, so the nozzle exits sit at the real tail. Behind the exhaust plane only a fin-root fairing and tailplane strakes carry on. On the twin-engine deck jets the engine bays close in on the nozzles as round boat-tail fairings, with flat tail booms carrying the fins and tailplanes. Side-by-side nozzles are sized from their real spacing so they never overlap, and 2D nozzles are rectangular. (3) Intakes are rounded trunks blended into the skin with raked lip rings; the F-22 / Su-57 have caret mouths cut into the chine. Wing sections are NACA teardrops, and cranked wings bend their outer panels to the registry's `outerWingDihedral` (with `kinkSpan`). (4) The airframe uses a skin shader (`Models.skinMat`) whose panel seams follow the structure, like real flush-riveted stressed skin: long stretched strips with continuous fore-and-aft seams on the fuselage, spanwise spar and hinge seams on the wings, and only sparse, faint staggered butt joints across them, with soft weathering instead of blocky panel tones. Seams and rivets fade out with distance, and fabric aircraft get rib tapes instead. The shader also varies the tone of adjacent panels and paints period camouflage: RAF disruptive, Luftwaffe splinter and mottle, WW1 lozenge fabric, USAF SEA, modern two- and three-tone greys, and stealth greys. National markings are wrapped onto the curved skin like decals. Radiators (`radiator`: belly scoops, chin radiators, underwing baths, wing-root inlets) and swing-wing gloves (`glove`) are explicit registry blocks with real dimensions. The builder also runs an explicit anomaly checklist; for example, `side_box` intakes flatten the fuselage sides into hard panels. Each rule that fires is logged in `Airframe.anomalies[id]`. `tools/tests/meshcheck.mjs` checks the registry (complete, valid, missing properties rejected) and that every part of every aircraft is watertight and faces outwards (`LOG=f22a,...` prints the checklist). Period details: open / framed / bubble / glazed cockpits with pilots, navigation lights, anti-glare panels, RAF roundel types and fin flashes, French fin flashes, US star-and-bar, outlined hinomaru.
- **Flight model** (`js/flight.js`): thrust, drag and climb are derived from each aircraft's real top speed, climb rate, turn time, roll rate and G limit. Lift depends on angle of attack, and the wing stalls past the critical angle. Turning bleeds speed, piston engines lose power at altitude, and controls stiffen in very fast dives.
- **Damage** (`js/damage.js`): there are no health bars. Each aircraft has hit boxes for engines, fuel tanks, crew, ammunition, wings, tailplane, fin and fuselage. Damaged engines lose power, smoke, seize or catch fire. Fuel tanks leak (self-sealing tanks close the holes) and can burn or explode. Wings lose lift, which makes the aircraft roll, and can snap off. A dead pilot means a dead aircraft. High-explosive shells spread fragments to neighbouring parts; armour plate stops small rounds from behind. The aircraft-condition panel shows the hit boxes coloured by state.
- **Aircraft condition panel:** the aircraft's own plan view, drawn from its airframe spec: fuselage, wings, tailplanes, fins, nacelles and booms. Each part is coloured by the state of its damage component, and engines, fuel tanks, ammunition and crew are marked where they sit.
- **Kill credit** (`Match.resolveKill`): when an aircraft is shot down (crew killed, or the aircraft explodes or breaks up), the enemy that did the most damage gets the kill. When it crashes with ≥10% damage, the top damage dealer gets it. Below 10%, credit goes to an enemy that hit it in the last 20 s, otherwise to the enemy aircraft closest to the crash.
- **Missiles** (`js/combat.js`): each type has its real length, diameter, motor burn time (boost and sustainer or ramjet) and active-seeker range (`MISSILE_PHYS` in `js/data.js`). Drag comes from the airframe and the air density, the fins can only pull as much G as the speed and height allow, and turning costs energy; guidance is proportional navigation. Heat seekers can be decoyed by flares (better seekers less often) and older ones only see the tailpipe. Semi-active radar missiles need the launching aircraft's radar to keep the target within its scan; active radar missiles fly on datalink updates (or inertially) until their own radar switches on near the target. A target flying across the radar beam close to the ground clutter (a notch) can break the lock, and chaff helps a lot then. Warnings only come from equipment the aircraft has: a radar warning receiver (Cold War and later jets) hears radar locks and radar-guided missiles, while a missile approach warner (F-22, F-35, Typhoon, Rafale, Su-35S, Su-57) sees motor plumes, so it also warns of heat seekers.
- **Bombs** fall with drag from their real diameter and mass (rising near the speed of sound); Snakeyes open retarding fins. The CCIP pipper, the bots and the bombs use the same fall model.
- **Bases** have AA matching the era: machine guns and "Archie" in WW1, light and heavy flak in WW2, radar guns and SAMs later. Their health bar appears when you get close.

## Online play
Online play is peer-to-peer over WebRTC (PeerJS), with join codes. Most home networks connect directly. Mobile data and school or office networks may need a relay (TURN) server: a free one (ExpressTURN or Metered) can be added in `js/netconfig.js`. The game traffic is encrypted end to end either way; a relay only forwards it.
